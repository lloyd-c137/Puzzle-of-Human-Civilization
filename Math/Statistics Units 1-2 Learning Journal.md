#### Subject and topic

Statistics — Classifying the shapes of quantitative distributions

#### Source or activity

Khan Academy video: “Classifying shapes of distributions.”

#### What I already understood

I understood that graphs can look different, but I was not yet sure how to name the overall shape or how to tell left-skewed from right-skewed.

#### What I learned

- **Symmetric:** the left and right sides are roughly mirror images.
- **Left-skewed:** the long tail extends to the left; most values are toward the right.
- **Right-skewed:** the long tail extends to the right; most values are toward the left.
- **Bimodal:** the distribution has two clear peaks, or modes.
- **Approximately uniform:** the bars or values are spread fairly evenly, making the graph look roughly flat.
- The direction of skew is named by the **tail**, not by where the main pile of data is.
- A distribution can look somewhat symmetric and still be more precisely described as bimodal if it has two peaks.

#### My understanding now

I can first look for peaks, then inspect the tails. One balanced peak suggests symmetry. A tail pointing left means left-skewed, and a tail pointing right means right-skewed. Two high points mean bimodal, while nearly equal heights across the range suggest uniform.

#### Example, evidence, or application

In the video:

- Housefly lengths were approximately symmetric.
