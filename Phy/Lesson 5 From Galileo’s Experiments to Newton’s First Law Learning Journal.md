### Learning record — 2026-09-01 — Galileo’s two-inclined-plane experiment

#### question

Galileo 的双斜面实验是什么

#### respond

Galileo placed two inclined planes facing each other and released a ball from rest on one side. The ball rolled down the first plane and then up the second plane, nearly reaching its original height; friction prevented it from reaching exactly the same height. When the slope of the second plane was made smaller, the ball had to travel farther to reach the same height. Galileo reasoned that if the second plane became horizontal and friction were absent, the ball would never need to climb and would continue moving indefinitely at constant speed in a straight line. This was evidence for inertia and against the idea that a continuous push is required to keep an object moving.

#### reflection

The learner is asking for the experiment before explaining its observation–reasoning–conclusion chain. The key boundary is that the ball’s endless motion on a horizontal plane is an ideal inference: real friction prevents it, so the experiment supports the conclusion by reducing resistance and extrapolating to zero friction.

### Learning record — 2026-09-01 — Newton’s first law: correcting the condition

#### question

当运动中的物体的速度突然消失时，物体会保持原有的速度做匀速直线运动

#### respond

The learner is aiming at the correct result but used the wrong condition. Newton’s first law applies when the net external force is zero, not when the object’s velocity disappears. If the velocity becomes zero, the object is at rest. If an external force stops acting while the object is already moving, its existing velocity remains unchanged, so it continues in uniform straight-line motion.

#### reflection

The learner understands the predicted motion but needs to distinguish velocity from force. The next step is to restate the law using “合外力为 0” and “保持原有速度,” including both the rest and moving cases.
