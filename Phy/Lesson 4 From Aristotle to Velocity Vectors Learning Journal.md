### Learning record — 2026-08-30 — Galileo’s concept of inertia

### Learning record — 2026-08-30 — Chapter 3 Q9: Acceleration calculation

#### question

100/10 = 10km2/h

#### respond

The numerical value 10 is correct, but the unit must be written as \(\mathrm{km/(h\cdot s)}\), because acceleration is a change in speed per unit time:

\[
a=\frac{100\ \mathrm{km/h}}{10\ \mathrm{s}}=10\ \mathrm{km/(h\cdot s)}.
\]

The unit \(\mathrm{km^2/h}\) is not correct because the calculation divides speed by time; it does not multiply two distances.

#### reflection

The learner can calculate the numerical acceleration but needs to track the units through the division. The next step is to recognize that \(\mathrm{km/h/s}\) means \(\mathrm{km/(h\cdot s)}\), and optionally convert the result to \(\mathrm{m/s^2}\).

### Learning record — 2026-08-30 — Meaning of the acceleration unit km/(h·s)

#### question

10km（h·s）是什么意思

#### respond

\(10\ \mathrm{km/(h\cdot s)}\) means that the speed increases by \(10\ \mathrm{km/h}\) every second. It is the same as \(10\ (\mathrm{km/h})/\mathrm{s}\). It is an acceleration unit, not a distance unit. In SI units:

\[
10\ \mathrm{km/(h\cdot s)}\approx2.78\ \mathrm{m/s^2}.
\]

#### reflection

The learner needs to read the unit as “speed change per second.” The numerical value describes how quickly the car’s speed changes, while the unit shows the two-stage division by hours and seconds.
