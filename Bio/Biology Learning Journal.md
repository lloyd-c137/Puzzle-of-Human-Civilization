## Learning logs

### Unit 1 — Organization of natural systems

**Learned:**

- Organism → species → population → community → ecosystem → biome → biosphere.
- A community contains living populations and their interactions.
- An ecosystem contains a community plus its abiotic environment.
- A biome is a large climate region; the biosphere is the global life-supporting zone.
- Atmosphere, hydrosphere, geosphere, and biosphere are connected Earth systems.

**Corrections:**

- A population is members of one species living in an area, not the area itself.
- A community can include interactions, but it does not include the abiotic environment as a component; an ecosystem does.
- A habitat is where an organism lives; a niche is how it lives and its role.

**Question:** Why is it called a community if communication is not required?  
**Answer:** Ecological interaction does not require language. Predation, competition, pollination, and decomposition are interactions.

### Unit 1 — Distribution of life

**Learned:** Latitude, temperature, precipitation, elevation, sunlight, water depth, salinity, and currents affect where organisms live.

**Corrections and examples:**

- Higher elevation usually means lower temperature; organisms may move upward when warming makes lower elevations unsuitable.
- A macroinvertebrate is an animal without a backbone that is large enough to see without a microscope.
- “Estimated” means approximate, not exact. A graph estimated a lower tolerance limit near 15%, so 20% was the closest answer.

### Unit 1 — An organism's niche

**Learned:**

- Tolerance is the ability to survive variation in an environmental factor.
- Optimal conditions support high survival and reproduction.
- Stressful conditions are near tolerance limits; some organisms may survive.
- Extreme conditions are beyond tolerance limits; none survive.
- Abundance means the number of individuals of a species in an area.
- A fundamental niche is the full possible niche; a realized niche is the part actually used after interactions and resources are considered.
- Specialists have narrow niches; generalists have broad niches.

**Mistake and correction:**

- In the peace lily question, I chose low abundance, few survivors, and stressful conditions for the point at zero abundance. The correction was no abundance, no survivors, and extreme conditions.

### Unit 4 — Population growth and carrying capacity

**Learned:**

- Population density is the number of individuals per area; dispersion is their arrangement.
- Clumped means grouped; uniform means evenly spaced; random means unpredictable spacing.
- Births and immigration add individuals. Deaths and emigration remove individuals.
- Exponential growth is J-shaped; logistic growth is S-shaped.
- Carrying capacity (K) is the maximum population size an environment can support. Population size may fluctuate above and below K.
- Limiting factors include resources, competition, predation, disease, parasitism, temperature, drought, floods, and fires.
- Density-dependent factors become stronger as a population becomes crowded. Density-independent factors affect populations regardless of density.
- Parasitism can lower carrying capacity by reducing host survival or reproduction. An ectoparasite lives on the outside of its host.
- r-selected and K-selected traits form a spectrum. r-selected species generally reproduce quickly with many offspring; K-selected species generally live longer, produce fewer offspring, and provide more care. Both can show exponential or logistic growth.
- Yeast is a microscopic fungus often used to study population growth.

**Mistakes and corrections:**

- For population growth, I chose emigration greater than immigration. The correction is immigration greater than emigration because immigration adds individuals and emigration removes them.
- When births and deaths are both 18 per 100 leopards, the current population count remains stable. Age structure can affect future growth, but it does not change the current count in that question.
- For carrying capacity, I chose that limiting factors do not affect it. The correction is that carrying capacity is affected by both resource availability and limiting factors. The correct statement was that population size often fluctuates above and below K.

**Current understanding:** Population growth depends on individuals entering and leaving, resource limits, environmental conditions, and interactions among organisms.
